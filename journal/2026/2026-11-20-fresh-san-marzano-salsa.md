# Fresh San Marzano Salsa: cook #1

**Verdict:** 5/5. The vegetables went in by count instead of weight, two-thirds of them sautéed and one-third raw. It tasted like a good jarred salsa, only fresh.

## In Brian's words
> i made the salsa... i ended up pretty much disregarding the weights of the veggies and adding the whole jalapeno, medium white onion and half the bell pepper... i sauteed 2/3 of them all mixed together to soften them, and kept 1/3 raw for texture, and it was delicious, highly recommend.

> honestly 5 it was like a jarred salsa but fresh/good lol

## What we did
- Followed recipe v1, but with the vegetables measured by count instead of weight. Nothing was weighed, so the actual grams are unknown.
  - **Onion:** 1 whole medium white onion (recipe: 150g, ~½ medium)
  - **Red bell pepper:** ½ pepper (recipe: 100g, ~⅔ medium)
  - **Jalapeño:** 1 whole (same as the recipe)
- Diced all three and mixed them together. Sautéed about ⅔ of the mix in the 2qt Made In saucier until soft, then set it aside. The fat, heat level, and time weren't recorded. Kept the other ⅓ raw.
- Blended the 794g San Marzanos with only the seasonings. No vegetables went into the blender; v1 had blended the jalapeño with the tomatoes.
- Mixed the cooked and raw vegetables into the blended tomatoes.
- Everything else followed the recipe: the onion rinse, 1.5g garlic powder, 2.5g True Lime, 8g coarse sea salt, and the full can of juice.

## How it turned out
5/5: "delicious, highly recommend." The sweetness and texture came across like a good jarred salsa, but fresh. The salt, the True Lime, and the full can of juice all worked as written. That answers two of the three questions v1 left for cook #1. The third, the onion's actual weight, is still open.

## Diagnosis
- **Cooking two-thirds of the vegetables is why the bigger onion worked.** v1 capped the onion at 150g because all of it went in raw, and cut raw onion keeps getting harsher as it sits. Heat stops those reactions and brings out the onion's sweetness, so a whole onion came across sweet rather than sharp.
- **The cooked portion explains the "jarred" taste.** Jarred salsas are cooked. The softened vegetables gave this one that mellow, sweet note, while the uncooked tomatoes and the raw third kept it tasting fresh.
- **Mixing the vegetables in instead of blending them** kept them in distinct pieces against a smooth base.
- **The seasoning held up.** Even with more vegetables than v1, it didn't need any extra salt or True Lime.
