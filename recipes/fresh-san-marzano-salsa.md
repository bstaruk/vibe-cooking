# Fresh San Marzano Salsa

> A restaurant-style table salsa made from one can of San Marzanos. The tomatoes are blended on low. Two-thirds of the onion, pepper, and jalapeño are softened in a pan and the rest stays raw, so every bite gets both sweetness and crunch.

**Serves** 8 · **Active** 25 min · **Total** 40 min · **Status** seasoned · v2

## Why this works

- **Canned tomatoes go in raw, with their juice.** They're already cooked soft, so they blend straight from the can, the way Bayless uses canned tomatoes in his rustic salsa. Blending on the lowest speed matters: Martínez warns that high speed whips in air and turns salsa pale, foamy, and smoothie-like.
- **Two-thirds of the vegetables are cooked, and one-third stays raw.** Cut raw onion keeps generating harsh sulfur compounds as it sits (McGee). A few minutes of heat stops that and brings out the onion's sweetness, which is why a whole onion works here. The raw third is rinsed first, as Bayless does with raw onion, and keeps the crunch. Only the tomatoes go in the blender, so the vegetables stay in distinct pieces. On cook #1 the result tasted like a good jarred salsa, only fresh.
- **One jalapeño, ribs kept.** Capsaicin sits in the ribs (the placenta). Cook's Illustrated measured ~100× the capsaicin of the flesh there, versus ~15× in the seeds. Spread across ~1.1kg of salsa, one jalapeño is mild to medium, so this keeps all of it.
- **House swaps, salted from zero.** None of the trusted versions uses bell pepper; it's our addition, for sweetness and crunch. Garlic powder stands in for fresh garlic, and True Lime for fresh lime (the maker says 2 packets taste like ~15g (1 tbsp) of juice). The can has no added salt (20mg sodium per 114g), so every gram of salt is ours: 8g is ~0.7% of the batch.

## Equipment

- **210mm Takamura gyuto**: fine dice. Any of the knives works; a thin blade makes the ⅛" (3mm) dice easier.
- **1.5qt All-Clad bowl**: rinse the onion
- **2qt Made In saucier**: soften two-thirds of the vegetables
- **3qt All-Clad bowl**: blend and mix. ~1.1kg fills it a bit over a third of the way.
- **Vitamix immersion blender**: speed 1 only
- **OXO scale**: tomatoes and vegetables
- **Timemore scale**: oil, salt, garlic powder, True Lime
- Cutting board, paper towels

## Ingredients

- **~225g** white onion (1 medium), ⅛" (3mm) dice
- **~70g** red bell pepper (½ medium), stem, seeds, and white ribs removed, ⅛" (3mm) dice
- **~30g** jalapeño (1), stemmed, ribs and seeds kept, minced
- **10g** olive oil (~2 tsp)
- **794g** Wegmans whole San Marzano tomatoes (one 28 oz can), with their juice
- **1.5g** garlic powder (~½ tsp)
- **2.5g** True Lime crystallized lime (~3 packets, or ~¾ tsp from the shaker), plus up to 1g (~1 packet) more to taste
- **8g** coarse sea salt (~1⅓ tsp), plus up to 2g more to taste

## Method

### Knife work

1. **Dice and rinse the onion.** Cut ~225g white onion (1 medium) into ⅛" (3mm) dice. Put it in the 1.5qt All-Clad bowl, cover with cold water, and swish for **about 30 sec**, until the water turns cloudy. Pour off the water, holding the onion back with a flat hand, then repeat once. Spread the onion on paper towels and blot dry.
2. **Dice the pepper.** Cut ~70g red bell pepper (½ medium; stem, seeds, and white ribs removed) into ⅛" (3mm) dice.
3. **Mince the jalapeño.** Stem the ~30g jalapeño, keep the ribs and seeds, and mince it fine. For a milder salsa, cut out the ribs and seeds. Wash your hands and the knife right away; capsaicin on fingers finds eyes.
4. **Mix and split.** Toss the ~225g onion, ~70g red bell pepper, and ~30g jalapeño together on the board (~325g in all). Set about a third (**~110g**) aside to go in raw. The other two-thirds (**~215g**) gets cooked.

### Soften

5. **Sauté two-thirds.** Heat 10g olive oil in the 2qt Made In saucier on the small zone over medium heat until it shimmers, **about 1 min**. Add the ~215g mixed vegetables and cook, stirring often, **5–7 min**, until the onion is soft and translucent but not browned. Pull the saucier off the heat and let it cool while you blend.

### Blend

6. **Open the can.** Pour 794g San Marzano tomatoes and all their juice into the 3qt All-Clad bowl. Fish out the basil leaf and any tough stem-end cores.
7. **Season.** Add 1.5g garlic powder, 2.5g True Lime (~3 packets), and 8g coarse sea salt.
8. **Blend low.** Tilt the bowl so the Vitamix immersion blender's bell guard stays fully under the surface. Then, on **speed 1**, pulse in **1–2 sec** bursts, about **8–10 pulses**, until no whole tomato pieces remain and it looks like a coarse purée. Stop before the color lightens or foam forms on top.

### Finish

9. **Fold in the vegetables.** Scrape the ~215g softened vegetables and their oil from the 2qt Made In saucier into the blended tomatoes in the 3qt All-Clad bowl. Then fold in the ~110g raw vegetables.
10. **Rest.** Rest **15 min**, on the counter or in the fridge. This gives the coarse salt and lime crystals time to dissolve, so the next taste is accurate.
11. **Taste and adjust.** Taste on a chip if you're serving it with salted chips. If it tastes flat, stir in up to 2g more coarse sea salt. If it tastes dull, stir in up to 1g more True Lime (~1 packet). Wait **about 2 min**, until no crystals crunch, then taste again.

## Make ahead & storage

- **Fridge:** covered, at **40°F (4°C)** or below. It's best within 2 days, while the raw third is still crisp. Extension guidance for fresh salsa is 5–7 days, never more than a week.
- **Make ahead:** the softened vegetables and blended base (steps 5–9, without the raw third) keep up to 2 days in the fridge. For the crispest result, dice the raw third (about ⅓ of the onion, pepper, and jalapeño) fresh and fold it in shortly before serving. Raw onion that sits in the salsa gets harsher; the cooked part doesn't.
- **Freezer:** safe. Freeze the blended base with the cooked vegetables, before the raw third goes in, because thawed raw onion and pepper go soft. Thaw in the fridge, then fold in freshly diced raw vegetables.
- **Don't can it.** Onion and pepper are low-acid, and this is an untested home recipe, so it isn't safe for water-bath canning.

## Riffs

- *(untried)* **More heat:** in step 4, add a minced yellow habanero to the mix, or use 2–3 jalapeños.
- *(untried)* **Fresh lime and cilantro:** in step 7, use 22g fresh lime juice (~1½ tbsp, about ½ lime) instead of the 2.5g True Lime. In step 9, fold in 8g cilantro leaves and tender stems (~½ cup), chopped, with the raw vegetables.
- *(untried)* **Fresh garlic:** in step 7, use 6g garlic (2 cloves), finely grated, instead of the 1.5g garlic powder. Raw garlic is sharper than powder, so start low.
- *(untried)* **Charred:** instead of the sauté in step 5, char the onion, jalapeño, and bell pepper in large pieces in a foil-lined Lancaster No. 10 skillet until blackened in spots and softened, the way Martínez does it. Then blend them with the tomatoes in step 8. It comes out smokier, and smooth rather than chunky.
