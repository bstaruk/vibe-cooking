# Fresh San Marzano Salsa

> A raw, restaurant-style table salsa made from one can of San Marzanos. The tomatoes are blended on low, then hand-diced onion and red bell pepper go in for crunch.

**Serves** 8 · **Active** 20 min · **Total** 35 min · **Status** raw · v1

## Why this works

- **Canned tomatoes go in raw, with their juice.** They're already cooked soft, so they blend straight from the can, the way Bayless uses canned tomatoes in his rustic salsa. Blending on the lowest speed matters: Martínez warns that high speed whips in air and turns salsa pale, foamy, and smoothie-like.
- **The onion is diced and rinsed, never blended.** Cut raw onion keeps generating harsh sulfur compounds as it sits (McGee). A cold rinse washes off what's on the surface (Bayless rinses raw onion for the same reason), and the knife keeps it crunchy.
- **One jalapeño, ribs kept.** Capsaicin sits in the ribs (the placenta). Cook's Illustrated measured ~100× the capsaicin of the flesh there, versus ~15× in the seeds. Spread across ~1.1kg of salsa, one jalapeño is mild to medium, so this keeps all of it.
- **House swaps, salted from zero.** None of the trusted versions uses bell pepper; it's our addition, for sweetness and crunch. Garlic powder stands in for fresh garlic, and True Lime for fresh lime (the maker says 2 packets taste like ~15g (1 tbsp) of juice). The can has no added salt (20mg sodium per 114g), so every gram of salt is ours: 8g is ~0.75% of the batch.

## Equipment

- **210mm Takamura gyuto**: fine dice. Any of the knives works; a thin blade makes the ⅛" (3mm) dice easier.
- **1.5qt All-Clad bowl**: rinse the onion
- **3qt All-Clad bowl**: blend and mix. ~1.1kg fills it a bit over a third of the way.
- **Vitamix immersion blender**: speed 1 only
- **OXO scale**: tomatoes and vegetables
- **Timemore scale**: salt, garlic powder, True Lime
- Cutting board, paper towels

## Ingredients

- **150g** white onion (~½ medium), ⅛" (3mm) dice
- **100g** red bell pepper (~⅔ medium), stem, seeds, and white ribs removed, ⅛" (3mm) dice
- **~30g** jalapeño (1), stemmed, ribs and seeds kept, minced
- **794g** Wegmans whole San Marzano tomatoes (one 28 oz can), with their juice
- **1.5g** garlic powder (~½ tsp)
- **2.5g** True Lime crystallized lime (~3 packets, or ~¾ tsp from the shaker), plus up to 1g (~1 packet) more to taste
- **8g** coarse sea salt (~1⅓ tsp), plus up to 2g more to taste

## Method

### Knife work

1. **Dice and rinse the onion.** Cut 150g white onion into ⅛" (3mm) dice. Put it in the 1.5qt All-Clad bowl, cover with cold water, and swish for **about 30 sec**, until the water turns cloudy. Pour off the water, holding the onion back with a flat hand, then repeat once. Spread the onion on paper towels and blot dry.
2. **Dice the pepper.** Cut 100g red bell pepper (stem, seeds, and white ribs removed) into ⅛" (3mm) dice.
3. **Mince the jalapeño.** Stem the ~30g jalapeño, keep the ribs and seeds, and mince it fine. For a milder salsa, cut out the ribs and seeds. Wash your hands and the knife right away; capsaicin on fingers finds eyes.

### Blend

4. **Open the can.** Pour 794g San Marzano tomatoes and all their juice into the 3qt All-Clad bowl. Fish out the basil leaf and any tough stem-end cores.
5. **Season.** Add the ~30g minced jalapeño, 1.5g garlic powder, 2.5g True Lime (~3 packets), and 8g coarse sea salt.
6. **Blend low.** Tilt the bowl so the Vitamix immersion blender's bell guard stays fully under the surface. Then, on **speed 1**, pulse in **1–2 sec** bursts, about **8–10 pulses**, until no whole tomato pieces remain and it looks like a coarse purée flecked with jalapeño. Stop before the color lightens or foam forms on top.

### Finish

7. **Stir in the crunch.** Fold the 150g rinsed onion and 100g diced red bell pepper into the salsa in the 3qt All-Clad bowl.
8. **Rest.** Rest **15 min**, on the counter or in the fridge. This gives the coarse salt and lime crystals time to dissolve, so the next taste is accurate.
9. **Taste and adjust.** Taste on a chip if you're serving it with salted chips. If it tastes flat, stir in up to 2g more coarse sea salt. If it tastes dull, stir in up to 1g more True Lime (~1 packet). Wait **about 2 min**, until no crystals crunch, then taste again.

## Make ahead & storage

- **Fridge:** covered, at **40°F (4°C)** or below. It's best within 2 days, while the onion and pepper are crisp. Extension guidance for fresh salsa is 5–7 days, never more than a week.
- **Make ahead:** blend the base (steps 4–6) up to 2 days ahead and refrigerate it. Do the knife work (steps 1–3) and stir in the onion and pepper shortly before serving. Raw onion that sits in the salsa gets harsher.
- **Freezer:** safe, but thawed raw onion and pepper go soft and the salsa weeps. If you freeze any, freeze only the blended base.
- **Don't can it.** Onion and pepper are low-acid, and this is an untested home recipe, so it isn't safe for water-bath canning.

## Riffs

- *(untried)* **More heat:** in step 5, add a minced yellow habanero from the garden, or use 2–3 jalapeños.
- *(untried)* **Fresh lime and cilantro:** in step 5, use 22g fresh lime juice (~1½ tbsp, about ½ lime) instead of the 2.5g True Lime. In step 7, stir in 8g cilantro leaves and tender stems (~½ cup), chopped, with the onion.
- *(untried)* **Fresh garlic:** in step 5, use 6g garlic (2 cloves), finely grated, instead of the 1.5g garlic powder. Raw garlic is sharper than powder, so start low.
- *(untried)* **Charred:** char the onion, jalapeño, and bell pepper (in large pieces) in a foil-lined Lancaster No. 10 skillet until blackened in spots and softened, the way Martínez does it. Then blend everything in step 6. It comes out smokier and sweeter, and it holds better, because cooked onion stops turning harsh.
